I am a PhD student passionate about making AI systems safer, more capable, and more accessible. My research spans AI safety, LLM evaluation, model merging, collaborative learning, and open-source AI development. I believe in building AI that serves everyone.

About Me

I'm a PhD student working at the intersection of AI safety, large language model capabilities, and democratic AI development. My mission is to ensure that powerful AI systems are safe, accessible, and beneficial for everyone.

Before focusing on AI safety, I spent two years working on AI for mental health, which deepened my commitment to building AI that genuinely helps people. This experience shaped my belief that AI development should prioritize human wellbeing above all else.

I'm a proud contributor to the open-source AI community, including the MergeKit library, and I actively work to democratize AI through research, mentorship, and community building.

Research

My research focuses on making AI systems safer, more capable, and more accessible to all.

AI Safety & Robustness

Developing methods to evaluate and improve the safety of large language models, including adversarial robustness and alignment.

LLM Capabilities & Evaluation

Creating better evaluation frameworks for understanding what language models can and cannot do, with a focus on probabilistic assessment.

Model Merging

Exploring techniques to combine multiple models efficiently, enabling collaborative model development without sharing data.

Collaborative Learning

Investigating methods for distributed and federated learning that preserve privacy while enabling collective intelligence.

Open-Source LLM Development

Contributing to and advocating for open-source AI development to ensure broad access to AI capabilities.

Democracy in AI

Working toward AI governance structures that give diverse stakeholders a voice in how AI systems are developed and deployed.

Current Projects

Probabilistic Evaluation of Attack Success Rates

Developing improved methods for evaluating adversarial attack success rates in probabilistic space, moving beyond binary success/failure metrics to capture the nuanced landscape of model vulnerabilities.
